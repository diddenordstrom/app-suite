# How marathon and half-marathon methods shape the last weeks, and what studies say

Gathered on 2026-11-24 for the [race-specific phase](../decisions/fartlek-race-specific-phase.md). Caveat: full texts and the books' week tables could not be opened from the session; most figures come from abstracts, vendor support pages and secondhand summaries. Treat numbers as typical values.

## Methods

- **Pfitzinger** (Advanced Marathoning, 12 or 18 weeks, 55–85+ miles a week): endurance → lactate threshold + endurance → race preparation → 3-week taper (about −20, −40, −60 %). 5–7 runs of 29–35 km from 13 to 3 weeks out; a midweek medium-long run (18–24 km); MP long runs (for example 32 km with 19 km at MP); LT runs at 15K–HM pace. Two quality days plus the medium-long run; a recovery week every 3–4 weeks.
- **Daniels** (2Q, 18 weeks): two Q sessions a week, one a long run with M blocks (3 km E + 13 km M + 2 km E), one T/I. Long run at most the lesser of 25–30 % of the week and about 150 minutes.
- **Hansons:** 6 runs, 3 "something of substance" a week; speed gives way to strength around week 10 (3×3.2 km at MP − 10 s/mile); a weekly MP tempo of 8 → 16 km; long run capped at 26 km, run on tired legs; taper about 10 days.
- **Canova** (elite): fundamental → special → specific (6–8 weeks at 95–105 % of race speed). Progressive long runs from 95 % to 102–105 % of MP, alternations (1 km at 103 % / 1 km at 90–95 %), special-block days about 9, 6 and 3 weeks out.
- **Norwegian / sub-threshold:** controlled threshold intervals all through, an MP long run added 6–8 weeks out; double threshold days only at 80–120+ km a week.
- **Apps:**
  - Runna: 32 km at least once, ideally twice; long run +10–15 % a week, a deload every 4–5 weeks. Paces come from the estimated current race time, never a goal time, so a goal cannot be "unrealistic"; changes go 20–30 s at a time.
  - Garmin Coach: marathon long runs reportedly top out at 22–24 km (forum reports).
  - Higdon Novice 1: long run up to 32 km once, a step-back every third week, a 3-week taper.

## Common key sessions

From 12–10 weeks out the general LT/VO2 work gives way to race pace: MP inside the long run grows from about 8 to 16–20 km, and pace work moves from about 90 % towards 100 % of MP. The recurring sessions:

- the MP long run
- the progressive long run (last third rising to MP)
- the fast-finish long run (last 5–10 km at MP)
- alternations (12–20 km)
- the MP tempo (8–16 km)
- strength repeats slightly under MP
- a dress rehearsal 10–14 days out
- a tune-up race 8–4 weeks out

For the half the same shape is shorter: 6–8 weeks, HMP repeats and alternations, long runs of 18–24 km with a fast finish, a taper of 7–10 days.

## Studies

- **Long runs and volume:**
  - Fokkema et al. 2020 (MSSE, 441 marathoners): under 40 km a week went with slower times and over 65 with faster ones. The 30–35 km longest-run group was the reference: under 25 km went with slower times, and over 35 km added nothing. Higher volume and longer runs were not linked to more injuries.
  - Doherty, Keogh & Davenport 2020 (JSAMS, meta-regression of 85 studies): every training variable, including runs of 32 km or more, went with faster times. A 4:00 finish went with about 44 km a week.
  - Sub-elite plans peak their long run at 30–32 km whatever the weekly volume.
- **Critical speed:** Smyth & Muniz-Pumares 2020 (MSSE, over 25,000 Strava runners). CS from training data predicted marathon time with about 7.7 % error. Runners raced at 84.8 % of CS on average, from 93 % at 2:30 to 79 % at 6:00. A first half above 94 % of CS predicted a slowdown of more than 25 %.
- **Taper and overreaching:**
  - Bosquet 2007 (meta-analysis): the best taper is about 2 weeks with volume cut 41–60 % on an exponential curve, keeping intensity and frequency.
  - Aubry 2014 (MSSE, triathletes): acute fatigue without a drop in performance gave the largest supercompensation. Functional overreaching blunted the taper gain and raised the risk of infection.
  - Thomas, Mujika & Busso (modelling): more load before the taper pays only with a longer or deeper taper.
- **Injury:**
  - Nielsen 2014: more than +30 % over two weeks gave more distance-related injuries in novices.
  - Damsted 2019: weekly increases of 20–60 % gave more injuries than increases under 20 % before a half marathon.
  - Toresdahl 2022 (BJSM, NYC Marathon): days with an acute:chronic ratio over 1.5 were linked to injury, and the 10 % rule did not protect.
- **Phase length:** no peer-reviewed optimum was found. Coaches (McMillan) say 6–10 weeks.
